Seven Seconds

A Taiwanese scooter licence-test simulator that grew into three games on one site in a week: the eight-station scooter test, a nine-station heavy-motorcycle test, and a food-delivery run where every traffic ticket cites its article — plus a fourth mode, playing the examiner, that was withdrawn but is still on the server.

The seven dimensions

memoryTechnical Depth 8.5/10

Three simulations share one approach and are honest about its limits. The scooter is a kinematic bicycle model with an inverted-pendulum balance layer on top, stepped at a fixed 1/60 s with the unstable roll sub-stepped so it behaves the same from 30 to 120 Hz (engine.js:1, 337); the comments call it 'assisted balance, not a full motorcycle dynamics model', cite the paper the coupling is taken from, and take the vehicle's dimensions from a manufacturer's sheet (engine.js:35-36, 281-283, 299). The heavy-motorcycle mode adds gears and a stall rule (heavy/physics.js:48-54, 91-93) under the same disclaimer (heavy/physics.js:149). The delivery mode goes furthest: it records the rider's inputs four floats per step with a state checkpoint every 120 steps, and its replay is a deterministic re-simulation rather than a tape of poses (delivery/recording.js:8, 26, 49-55). Nothing is modelled or textured from files — riders, bikes, a 36-junction town and its landmarks are assembled from primitives and canvas-drawn atlases — and quality steps down by measured frame rate (scene.js:1187-1189, delivery/lighting.js:3-10). All three modes can turn a run into a share video on the device: WebCodecs H.264, a hand-written MP4 muxer, cuts locked to a 128.57 BPM track, nothing uploaded (highlight.js:1, 14). The limits are the ones the comments admit — top speed is 4.5 m/s (engine.js:46), there is no tyre model, the 'manual' heavy bike has no clutch — plus one they do not: in the heavy mode, hitting the examiner is detected in the renderer at display rate and then charged 32 points, which puts one deduction outside the deterministic engine (heavy/scene.js:400-403, heavy/app.js:477).

stadia_controllerGame Design 8.5/10

Each mode takes a real rulebook as its specification and says where it departs from it. The scooter test's rules page names the edition of the scoring sheet it follows and the date it was checked, then lists nine ways the simulation differs — its retry scoring, for one, is 'the game's arrangement, not the sheet's wording' (rules:77, 96-107; exam.js:37-38). The heavy-motorcycle mode holds the official sheet as one frozen table of 27 coded deductions (heavy/rules.js:6-35), states in the code that the sheet gives no course sizes so 'every number here is a game setting' (heavy/courses.js:2-3), and its rules page lists the six official items it does not judge and why (heavy/rules:92-101). The delivery mode turns traffic law into a receipt: ten violations, each with its article of the Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act and a fine (delivery/engine.js:30-41), a police line of sight that 'never' passes through a building (delivery/engine.js:241-247), and a rule that an unseen violation cannot be caught by the officer it summons (delivery/engine.js:300-301). Riding without having passed the scooter exam on the same device earns an NT$18,000 ticket — the modes are joined through the keepsake licence (delivery/delivery-license.js:2-5). Delivery deadlines are budgeted from a scripted rider 'measured over 6,400 orders' (delivery/engine.js:190-193). Reservations: the delivery mode's two-stage left turn is switched off (delivery/engine.js:21), every one of its '128 routes' is the same three-junction zig-zag (delivery/engine.js:102-104), and because the scooter test only deducts 8, 16 or 32 points, the lowest passing score is 76 and any 32-point item fails (share.js:115).

securityBackend & Security 6.5/10

The same small, careful surface as its sibling car game, with the same blind spot. Two same-origin endpoints exist: an anonymous event beacon and a counter read (stats.js:7, app.js:491). The beacon is switched off entirely under Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control (stats.js:185), events pass an allow-list cleaner, and the nickname never leaves the device. Response headers are strict — script-src 'self', object-src 'none', HSTS — and missing paths return a proper 404, which the car game did not manage. Across roughly 22,000 lines there are ten uses of innerHTML, all fed from literals or a constant icon table, and no eval. Shared links are parsed defensively: a delivery link accepts only a base-36 seed, a bounded amount and a known edition number, and 'nothing else is read from the address' (delivery/order-link.js:1-14). What holds the score down: the keepsake licences are issued entirely in the browser, so nothing ties a serial to a run that was actually ridden, and the public counters rest on an unauthenticated beacon whose only visible guard is a display floor so 'a hand-made beacon cannot claim a 10-second license' (stats.js:26-30). The public data statement is also narrower than the payload: the rules page mentions play counts, results and failure reasons, while the beacon also carries a random per-day device id, a session id, a referrer category and the device class (rules:114, stats.js:154). The Worker behind it is named in a comment but not visible (stats.js:1-5).

codeCode Quality 7/10

Good bones, uneven upkeep, and three codebases that have started to drift. The cores are pure and say so in their headers — the heavy engine is 'pure and deterministic: no DOM, no clock, no storage' (heavy/engine.js:1) — and dependencies are injected: storage, clock, audio context and video encoder are all parameters, which is what lets the modules load under Node. The heavy mode is the cleanest of the three, 41 files averaging under 180 lines. The root game is where the debt sits: app.js packs 66 KB into 492 lines, and scoring is keyed on Chinese display strings — the table that maps a finish reason to its deduction uses the sentence shown to the player as its key (engine.js:185-192), so a copy edit can change a score. Station names have already drifted between files (engine.js:18 against coach.js:5). Sharing between the modes is done by copying and the comments say so: 'keep the two in step by hand' (heavy/draw-kit.js:2). The MP4 muxer now exists three times; delivery/input.js and delivery/license-rules.js are byte-identical to root files; and delivery/vendor/ is a second complete copy of three.js. Cache-bust tokens are hand-named and applied inconsistently — ?v=ride-1 on 27 root imports, none at all in the two newer modes. And the withdrawn examiner mode, 3,195 lines in five files, is still deployed.

experimentTesting 4/10

No test file ships and no CI can be observed, so the score is capped as it is for the other shipped-artifact projects. Within that cap this is the strongest case: the comments name tests precisely and the code is shaped for them. The changelog's header says 'the test keeps the newest entry in step with package.json' (changelog.js:1-2); the stats module says 'the tests keep these in step with examiner-core.js' (stats.js:20); the examiner module notes that its upper half is pure functions testable under Node (examiner-mode.js:3). The examiner mode also contains a real self-check that does ship: an autopilot rides each generated examinee through the actual engine, and any whose result differs from its script is rejected (examiner-core.js:3-4). The delivery mode quotes results from a scripted rider across 6,400 orders (delivery/engine.js:190-193), and exports a state hash and diagnostics that nothing in the shipped code calls. An outsider can read none of the tests, run none of them, and count none of them.

verifiedCompleteness 9/10

Three finished products in seven days. The scooter test has eight practice stations and a full mock exam with one retry, a keepsake licence in three formats, a scrubbable replay with half speed, an instant replay after a miss, share cards with caption choices, challenge links, three camera views including first person, and an in-game changelog of 29 releases. The heavy-motorcycle mode repeats that set for nine stations and two transmissions, and adds an examiner who walks, takes notes and can be run over. The delivery mode adds a rotating minimap, an itemised receipt with evidence photos for each ticket, a replay with witness cameras, and same-order links that show what the sharer took home. Keyboard and multi-touch are both complete, reduced-motion is honoured in script as well as stylesheet, dialogs are native and labelled, and each mode handles WebGL loss and failed downloads with a message. Two gaps: there is no web app manifest or service worker although the code detects home-screen launch, and the scooter rules page still carries a 9 November date while describing features added afterwards.

emoji_objectsOriginality 8/10

This is the chassis the same author's car-test game was built on, so the credit for the frame belongs here: a public licence procedure modelled item by item, with the game's own inventions labelled as such. On top of that are three ideas that are hard to place in any genre. The delivery mode is a traffic-law game disguised as a gig-economy one, where the punchline is a receipt that converts fines into deliveries ridden for nothing. The modes are linked through an object rather than a menu — the licence earned in one is checked in another. And the withdrawn examiner mode reverses the roles: the player holds the stopwatch while seeded robot examinees ride the real engine, with a daily shift number and emoji share text in the manner of a daily word puzzle (examiner-core.js:3-4). The heavy-motorcycle mode is the least new of the three, an honest port of the same structure to a different sheet.

  • Tickets that cite their article — ten violations with statute references and fines, and enforcement that cannot see through buildings or catch what it did not witness (delivery/engine.js:30-41, 241-247, 300-301)

The mode that was put away

The changelog records that on 10 November the 'play the examiner' mode was 'put away for rework'. It never records the mode's launch: versions 2.6.0 and 2.6.1 appear only in code comments (stats.js:20), so the player-facing history shows a withdrawal with no arrival.

The code is all still there — five files, 3,195 lines, loaded only on demand — behind a single constant, EXAMINER_PAUSED=true (app.js:137). The comment above it describes 'a grey button for everyone'; the markup ships that button with the hidden attribute (index.html:146), and no code was found that removes it. There is also an owner's way in: a preview link whose token is checked against a SHA-256 kept in the client (app.js:139-140). It unlocks only the paused mode, locally.

It is worth describing because it is the most original thing on the site and currently nobody can play it. It is also a compact picture of how this project moves: a feature shipped, was pulled within a day, and was left in place under a flag while two entirely new games were built around it in the same week.

smart_toyAI-assisted development

No tool is named in any of the three modes, and the numbered plans, waves and rulings that fill the car game's comments are absent here. What the delivery mode does carry is a third-person owner: ticket values are 'the exact owner-supplied' table (delivery/engine.js:4), the muxer is 'adapted from the owner's' highlight code (delivery/video-codec.js:1), the scooter model is 'extracted from the owner's' scene and the rest is 'original to this spike' (delivery/models.js:3-4), and a stylesheet is 'copied from the read-only' main UI (delivery/shared-ui.css:1). That is how a contributor writes when working beside code they were told not to change — a second person or an agent. Which of the two cannot be determined from the files.

Verdict

The origin of the licence-simulator chassis, and in one week more than a simulator: three rule-faithful games that each state where they depart from the rulebook, joined by a keepsake licence, with deterministic replay and on-device video throughout. The speed shows in the seams — copied modules, scoring tied to display strings, a withdrawn mode still deployed — and, as with its sibling, nothing an outsider could use to check the tests or the server.
