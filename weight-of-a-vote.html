Overview / Kōritsu Lab (Ion)

Weight of a Vote

A Taipei mayoral campaign game set on the dates of nine real elections from 1994 to 2026, with invented candidates and unnamed parties: ten turns of six hours each as candidate, staffer or someone at the bottom of the ladder, while every compromise rewrites, in red pen, the sentence you wrote down at the start. Seven elections are open; the 2026 one is scheduled to open six days before the real vote.

The seven dimensions

memoryTechnical Depth 8/10

The site publishes source maps with sourcesContent, so the original TypeScript could be read: 121 files and 29,867 lines, of which the rules engine is 22 files and 4,499 lines with no DOM access — its header says it is shared by the UI and a simulator (src/engine/index.ts:1). Votes are computed as population group × turnout × support, with support a distribution over candidates that sums to one (src/engine/votes.ts:1, src/engine/election.ts:18-20). Every vote-moving effect passes a swing cap that applies it once, measures the movement, restores a snapshot and re-applies it scaled by how far the race has already moved (src/engine/votes.ts:50-66); the late transfer of votes away from trailing candidates and the count-night noise are separate steps (src/engine/votes.ts:90-138). All randomness comes from a mulberry32 generator whose single 32-bit state is stored in the save, and side systems draw from separately seeded streams so they do not disturb it (src/engine/rng.ts:1, src/pundit/show.ts:4). Era-specific rule modules register themselves only when that era's content is loaded (src/engine/eraHooks.ts:1-3, src/game/data.ts:81-85). Nothing seen or heard is an asset file: comic panels are composed as SVG with SMIL animation from about twenty scene types (src/art/scene.ts:1-3, 4,706 lines), and sound is synthesised with Web Audio (src/audio/ambience.ts:2). Limits: the simulator, build scripts, schemas and Worker are named but not shipped, and the vote model is a tuned abstraction, not one fitted to data.

stadia_controllerGame Design 8.5/10

The loop is a time budget that cannot be met: ten turns from 182 to 3 days before polling day, six hours each, with three to six events on the board (assets/data-Dz9aFdZw.js, turn:{hours:6,count:10,daysBefore:[182,152,121). Each event offers options with different hour costs and a stated consequence for leaving it alone; options that compromise add to a vow score, and at 2, 4, 6 and 8 the sentence the player wrote at the start is rewritten in red pen (same file, vow:{stages:[2,4,6,8]). Endings cross winning or losing with how much of that sentence survives (src/engine/election.ts:10-16). Each election has one signature rule drawn from what that campaign was like: goodwill that leaks away unless it is grounded (1998), supporters who feel the race is won and stay home (2002, src/engine/relax.ts:1-6), a scandal modelled as city-wide rain (2006), an exposition whose construction lag costs the incumbent (2010), online buzz (2014), and polling-station queues that soft supporters abandon (2018, src/engine/queue.ts:1-8). The third role changes every time — ward chief, taxi driver, phone pollster, talk-show pundit, exposition temp, student, paid rumour writer — and several are separate games with their own screens. Content runs to 3,082 events and about 750,000 Chinese characters across seven eras. One decision is written into the code: the three options of the 'crux' night carry the same odds of winning and only change who the player becomes (src/engine/turn.ts:168). Reservations: per-candidate multipliers such as rivalMul from 0.32 to 3.2 show how much hand-tuning holds the balance, and the win rates the author cites come from a simulator that is not published.

securityBackend & Security 6.5/10

A static front end on Cloudflare with a same-origin /api whose code cannot be seen; the client only imports its types from worker/core (src/stats/client.ts:5). The client posts to /visit, /funnel, /run, /me and /feedback and reads /stats. What is collected is disclosed in unusual detail: the privacy page, dated 2026-10-13, lists the per-run record — character name and appearance, typed text, choices, an anonymous code kept in the browser, county, source category and device class — and that matches statsPayload (src/game/run.ts:281-303). An opt-out switch stops every first-party post (src/stats/client.ts:9, 58), and Google Analytics is not loaded under Global Privacy Control, Do Not Track or the opt-out (index.html, n.globalPrivacyControl===true||n.doNotTrack==='1'). The record-transfer link keeps its codes in the URL fragment, removes them before analytics run, and treats the server's reply as untrusted, re-checking every field (src/game/linkMerge.ts:4). Player text is escaped before it becomes markup (src/game/run.ts:511, src/game/replay.ts:71-73), and there is no eval. Hygiene gaps: the home page sends no Content-Security-Policy and no HSTS header and does send access-control-allow-origin: *; unknown paths return an HTML page with status 200; the Cloudflare analytics beacon is a static tag that loads whatever the privacy signals say, and those signals do not stop the first-party posts, only the opt-out does; the anonymous code works as a bearer token for one's play history, which the policy states; and public source maps expose the whole source and internal document paths. Server-side validation, rate limiting (the client handles a 429 for feedback) and storage could not be examined.

codeCode Quality 7.5/10

Because the maps carry the original sources, this is one of the few artifact-only projects whose code can be judged as it was written. It is typed TypeScript around a pure core: across 121 files there is no any, no @ts-ignore and no lint suppression; state is plain JSON that 'can go straight into localStorage' (src/engine/state.ts:1); the clock is read in one place so tests can replace it (src/game/data.ts:38); and several modules declare themselves pure functions that import only types, to keep the first-load bundle from fragmenting (src/game/linkMerge.ts:2-3, src/game/turning.ts:1-2). Comments give reasons and record dated incidents, such as a soft-lock found in play on 2026-10-14 and how it was fixed (src/engine/turn.ts:208-209). Tunable numbers sit in one balance configuration instead of in code. The debts are just as readable. The third role is called chief everywhere although it now covers eight different jobs, so the result logic is a chain of branches on state.chief?.kind (src/engine/election.ts:88-106) and one file has to note that 'the role in code is chief' (src/engine/writer.ts:1). src/art/scene.ts is a single 4,706-line, 587 KB file; 1,878 lines in the codebase are longer than 200 characters; an esc helper is defined five times; more than 200 non-null assertions and 15 as unknown as casts mark where JSON enters without runtime checks. Compatibility with old saves is handled by optional fields annotated '舊存檔沒有' 54 times, not by versioned migration.

experimentTesting 4/10

No test file, simulator or CI configuration is shipped, so the score is capped as it is for the other artifact-only projects; within that cap the evidence is strong. Comments refer to tests as things that exist: the release logic is 'read by the menu, the build script and the tests' (src/game/release.ts:1); two modules share one rule and a comment says the test checks them against each other (src/game/replay.ts:17, src/game/nextTry.ts:26); exported helpers are marked as being for tests (src/audio/callAmb.ts:16, 1216). The engine is shaped to be driven without a browser — pure, seeded, no DOM — and a simulator is named with a file path, sim/caller.ts (src/engine/caller.ts:60). Two comments record bugs 'caught by simulation', one dated 2026-11-07 (src/ui/screens/Scene.tsx:47, src/engine/turn.ts:39). Content is checked at build time (src/schema/minigame.ts:2 names scripts/build-content.ts). The studio's journal says one role's simulated win rate was compared with real players' and that a wording change left the simulation's results unchanged. A QA hook, window.__mini, ships inside the mini-games (src/ui/games/common.ts:18). None of the tests can be read, run or counted from outside.

verifiedCompleteness 8.5/10

Seven of the nine elections are playable (1994 to 2018), each with three roles; 2026 is scheduled for 22 November and 2022 is marked only as coming soon (assets/data-Dz9aFdZw.js, comingSoon:{2022:). What is open is finished to an unusual degree: character creation with a parametric face studio and life cards, comic-panel diaries, three poll nights drawn in the era's own medium, a voting day that runs through ballot, count, epilogue and thank-you tour, an identity card, a comic book of the whole run, share images in post and story formats drawn on canvas with a QR code, a closing montage assembled from the player's own run, and a link for carrying records to another browser. Accessibility is deliberate: a skip link on the about and privacy pages, large-text and reduced-motion settings that default from the system preference (src/ui/settings.ts:5-12), animation stripped from the panels under reduced motion (src/art/scene.ts:2), and 339 aria- attributes in the sources. Deployment includes a landing page per era, a sitemap, llms.txt, an about page with a FAQ and a privacy policy; a tab left open across a deploy reloads itself once (src/ui/stale.ts:1-3). Gaps: a web app manifest without a service worker, so no offline play; voice lines fall back to browser speech synthesis until recordings exist (src/audio/voice.ts:1); music is one simple synthesised theme per era (src/audio/music.ts:1); and players see no changelog or version, the only version mark being a build stamp sent with feedback (assets/run-DUNFAf5i.js, 77a790b·2026-11-15).

emoji_objectsOriginality 8/10

Election-campaign simulators exist, and a board of events resolved turn by turn is a known form. What is new is where this game puts its question. Winning is paired with a sentence the player wrote, and compromises edit that sentence in red until, in the site's own example, 'let young people stay' has 'get elected first' written over it; the ending is titled on both axes. The second idea is that every election gets its own rule, taken from what that campaign was remembered for, and that one engine carries them as plug-in modules. The third is the roles at the bottom of the ladder, which are small games of their own: a pollster's night shift, dialling one number at a time and deciding whether to tick what people actually said (src/engine/caller.ts:1-4); a pundit whose ratings and whose ledger of true statements pull apart (src/pundit/show.ts:1); a paid writer assembling rumours from headline, picture and source fragments while someone tracing them closes in (src/engine/writer.ts:4-13). Setting all of it on the dates of real elections in one city, with invented characters who return, age and are succeeded by their children between eras (src/game/data.ts:34), has no close precedent that this review knows of. The presentation follows the same idea: each era's events arrive through its own medium, from pager and fax to group chat.

trending_upHighlights

  • One signature rule per election — complacent supporters, scandal as rain, polling-station queues and others, each a module loaded with its era (src/engine/relax.ts:1-6, src/engine/queue.ts:1-8, src/engine/eraHooks.ts:1-3)
  • The rewritten vow — compromise is scored separately from votes, crosses the result to give the ending, and on the decisive night changes only who the player becomes (src/engine/election.ts:10-16, src/engine/turn.ts:168)

  • Disclosure that matches the payload — the privacy page lists what a finished run sends, the code sends that, and one switch stops all first-party posts (src/game/run.ts:281-303, src/stats/client.ts:9, 58)
  • Switches built for the 2026 election period — result display, share images and the all-players tally each have a separate control (src/ui/screens/VoteDay.tsx:54-55, src/share/image.ts:102-105, src/ui/screens/IdCard.tsx:202-206)

The 2026 chapter and its switches

Eight of the nine elections in this game are history. The ninth is not: the configuration gives its date as electionDate: 2026-11-28, and the 2026 chapter is scheduled to open at 2026-11-22T20:00:00+08:00, six days earlier (assets/data-Dz9aFdZw.js). Its line-up already ships in the same file: two invented candidates whose seats are labelled '在野黨・現任市長' and '執政黨・挑戰者'. As everywhere in the game, parties have no names — the data calls them a, b and new — and every screen that shows a number says it is fictional ('遊戲內虛構民調', src/ui/screens/PollNight.tsx:396). The about page says the game borrows only the structure and atmosphere of each race and campaigns for no one; candidate biographies do follow the outline of their period under invented names.

The code prepares for that week with separate controls. results2026:{showInGame:!0,showOnShare:!1} lets a 2026 run show its count in the game while share images never state who won, and a comment adds that 2026 share images carry only the ending, 'not event images such as the deepfake video' (src/share/image.ts:3, 102-105). If showInGame is turned off, the run ends at the polling-station door (src/ui/screens/VoteDay.tsx:54-55). statsEmbargo:{2026: 2026-11-28T16:00:00+08:00 covers the tally of how all players voted: the client expects the server to withhold it and shows 'public after voting ends (4 pm, 28 November)' (src/ui/screens/IdCard.tsx:202-206). Support figures from the turns the game treats as a poll-ban period are not drawn in the 2026 comic book before a result exists (src/ui/screens/Book.tsx:37-38). The statistics endpoint labels its own output '遊戲玩家統計，不是民意調查'.

What cannot be seen is the other half. The embargo is enforced on a server whose code is not public; the one response fetched for this review, for 1994, carried embargoed:false. The shipped files do not say which rule or guideline each switch answers to, and this review takes no position on whether the arrangement is sufficient — it records that the author anticipated the question and built the controls before the chapter opened. The historical grounding is similarly bounded: real polling dates, round electorate figures and two- or three-way formats come from the record, while group sizes, starting support and tuning are game settings with no source cited, which is also what the site tells its players.

smart_toyAI-assisted development

The game itself carries no AI credit. The studio's own pages do: its about page says AI tools are used to assist with code and text and that everything is reviewed before publication, and a journal post dated 2026-10-24 says much of the game's text is drafted by AI and then reviewed line by line by the author. In the sources recovered from the maps, four files cite a 'sample page' at a claude.ai/artifact/ address (src/ui/screens/Shift.tsx:4, src/ui/screens/Phone.tsx:3, src/ui/components/YouNight.tsx:2, src/you/phone.ts:2), which names one tool. Comments refer to the author in the third person with dates about seventy times — '作者 10/16 同意' (src/pundit/show.ts:2) — and point to briefing files such as content/2018/plan-engine.md, described as instructions for the engine (src/engine/anger.ts:2). That reads like an assistant recording an owner's rulings; this is inference, not something the files state. One data point sits beside the review claim: every event carries a status field, and in the shipped bundles it reads reviewed for 343 events and draft for 2,739, including every event from 1998 to 2018. The runtime does not read the field and nothing shipped defines it, so it cannot show that those texts went unreviewed.

Verdict

A large and carefully built narrative simulation: a pure, seeded engine carrying a different rule for each of seven elections, three thousand events, hand-coded comic art and synthesised sound, and a privacy statement that matches what the code sends. Because its source maps are public, the code can be read as written, and it reads well apart from one overloaded role name and some very large files. What an outsider cannot check are the tests, the simulator behind its balance and the server; what a reader should know is that the model's numbers are settings, not data, and that the chapter set in 2026 is due to open during the real campaign with a set of purpose-built restraints.
