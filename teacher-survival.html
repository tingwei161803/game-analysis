Teacher Infinite Flow: A Workplace Survival Game

One school year in twelve rounds: four students from kindergarten to senior high, four lengths of service, and a hidden multiplier that makes caring cost more than the option says — written by a serving special-education teacher, in a single 2,397-line HTML file with no randomness and no save.

The seven dimensions

memoryTechnical Depth 5/10

The whole game is one unminified HTML file of 2,397 lines: about 435 lines of CSS, about 1,200 lines of story tables and roughly 620 lines of engine and screens, with no library, no build step and no asset other than a favicon. The most interesting piece of engineering is a small condition language. Each option may carry a lock built from any, all, flag, relief, iron, or a stat compared either with a fixed value or with the player's own starting value, and two short recursive functions consume the same structure: meets decides whether the option is open (index.html:1781-1793) and condText turns it into the sentence shown on a locked card, including the player's current count (index.html:1759-1778). The cost model is equally compact: burden builds a situational multiplier from thresholds, the school term and open 'doors', caps it at BURDEN_CAP, then multiplies by the character's fixed rate (index.html:1796-1810). There is no Math.random anywhere, so a run is fully determined by the choices made. The ceiling is scale and ambition: screens are built by string concatenation into innerHTML, state is one global object, nothing is persisted, there is no audio or animation, and the code solves no performance or concurrency problem.

stadia_controllerGame Design 8/10

The rules make an argument and the writing carries it. Every round offers a procedural answer, a relational one, a boundary and sometimes a locked 'third way' (index.html:509), and each option states its price in a single line of consequence. The price shown is not the price paid: losses to stamina and morale are multiplied by a seniority rate from 1.22 to 1.82 and by a strain factor that grows as the player weakens (index.html:1796-1810), and the following screen prints the real bill with its reasons (index.html:2017-2034). Debts persist. Hearing an upset parent out at length opens a 'door' that charges morale every round until it is shut (index.html:1833-1839), learning a student's secret costs one point a round thereafter (index.html:1840-1843), and a favour granted or refused in round two comes back as a no-choice 'bill' round in round ten (index.html:727-738). The first collapse buys one round of leave; the second ends the year (index.html:2146-2175). The prose is specific and restrained — a child who recognises his teacher by her lanyard, a mother with 1999 already typed on her phone. The limits: all four storylines share one twelve-beat skeleton with several options repeated word for word, nothing varies between runs of the same choices, and the first 'third way' option needs rapport of 56, which two of the four starting characters do not have (index.html:550, 482-507).

securityBackend & Security 5.5/10

There is no server of the game's own, no storage and no player input, but the page is not as offline as it says. The title screen's footer reads '離線單檔．無需連線．關掉就沒了' (index.html:1924) and the header comment repeats '離線單檔' (index.html:462), yet the first function to run, trackVisit, sends one request per page load to a Google Apps Script address carrying the tool's name, trying sendBeacon, then fetch with no-cors, then an image request (index.html:465, 1731-1744). The request carries no identifier and nothing is written to cookies or localStorage; a single GET made for this audit returned a small JSON acknowledgement. What the script behind that address records could not be seen, and the page has no privacy note. The second half of the footer is accurate: closing the tab loses everything. On posture, the home page is served with HSTS and no Content-Security-Policy, frame or content-type headers; missing paths return a real 404; there is no robots.txt. All markup written with innerHTML comes from constants in the file, and no URL parameter is read, so there is no injection surface to speak of. At fetch time the host was also appending its own badge script to the page.

codeCode Quality 6/10

The file is easy to read from top to bottom: tunable numbers sit in one commented CFG block (index.html:469-477), the four storylines follow an identical record shape, and the engine is a dozen short functions with Chinese section headers. Keeping the stories as data is what lets about 620 lines of logic drive four of them, and a parse of the tables for this audit found every flag named in a lock set somewhere and every 'cause' matched by a bill. The structure is still that of a script, not a program. Everything is global; render both charges the round's upkeep and draws the screen (index.html:1999-2005), so returning from leave on the final round charges upkeep a second time (index.html:2171-2172); the ending reads the last event by a literal index, S.L.r[11] (index.html:2263). There is residue: countPlay is an empty function still called twice (index.html:1746), S.log is appended to and never read (index.html:2128), and the dino, storm and role fields in the tables are not used by any code. Reveal text is duplicated verbatim between two options in the same round (index.html:625-626, 633-634), and the comment calling the file offline sits three lines above the counter address.

experimentTesting 1.5/10

Nothing is shipped and nothing is referenced: no test file, no harness, no CI, and no comment that mentions one; requests for package.json and README.md at the site root return 404, and no public repository was found. The game would be unusually cheap to test. It has no randomness, so any sequence of choices has exactly one outcome, and its content is plain data with a regular shape. A short script could check the tables for dangling flags or missing branches and replay fixed sequences to confirm that each ending, each student epilogue and each of the twelve supporting-witness options can be reached. This audit did a version of that by re-implementing the rules outside the page, which is how the balance observations elsewhere in this report were obtained; that re-implementation is this audit's own work, not something the project provides. The only thing in the file that resembles a check is defensive: the counter skips itself when its address is unset (index.html:1733).

verifiedCompleteness 7/10

A finished short game. The title screen explains the rules in three paragraphs and offers four characters and four storylines, sixteen starting combinations in all (index.html:1871-1948). Each storyline runs twelve rounds with 46 written options, a sticky panel of five meters, locked options that state their unlock conditions, a leave screen, and an ending that assembles an investigation result, a colleague's testimony, an epilogue for the chosen Wednesday, one of three epilogues for the student, a title for the player's style, a before-and-after table and a one-line summary of the three relationships (index.html:2261-2391). Players who collapsed or took leave are shown a short note telling them that real life has no such mechanic and to take the leave they need (index.html:2365-2373). The layout is a single 680-pixel column that works on a phone. What is missing: any save or resume, sound, a record of past runs or endings seen, sharing, and any page metadata beyond the title (no description, no social preview). Accessibility is partial: choices are real buttons and the page declares zh-Hant, but there are no ARIA attributes, and the grey consequence text measures about 3.6:1 against its card (index.html:254). The header says 'v9'; there is no changelog or date.

emoji_objectsOriginality 8/10

The subject is the author's own working life. The linked site describes them as a serving senior-high resource-class teacher, and the detail shows it: the 1999 citizen hotline used as a threat, the school incident review meeting as the final scene, evaluation files a principal wants 'made prettier', a student quietly signing his own contact book. Branching text games with meters are a long-established form, and the title's '無限流' — a web-fiction genre about surviving a series of deadly scenarios — is a label more than a structure: the only trace of it is the replay button's wording, '再走一次別人的一年' (index.html:2381). The original part is how the rules state a position. The option that looks kindest is the most expensive, the option that protects the teacher costs the student, the procedural option accumulates 'iron' evidence that later speaks for the teacher (index.html:2193-2197), and the investigation always clears the player — what changes is whether anyone stood beside them (index.html:2199-2205). No artwork, audio or third-party content is used. The closing line reads '故事是虛構的，那些選擇不是' (index.html:2384).

What the numbers argue

Because the game has no randomness, its rules can be replayed exactly. For this audit the rules were re-implemented outside the page from the source and run over all sixteen combinations of storyline and character. Three fixed strategies give three consistent results. Always choosing the relational answer ends in long-term sick leave in all sixteen, between rounds nine and twelve. Always choosing the boundary answer finishes all sixteen without ever needing leave and with more stamina and morale than at the start — and with the worst of the three student epilogues every time. Always choosing the procedural answer reaches the hearing with the 'iron evidence' result and morale at zero.

That is a position, stated in arithmetic: wholehearted care is not survivable as a policy, self-protection is, and the student pays the difference. The way out the game offers is the locked 'third way' options, and those open mainly through rapport and trust built earlier, so they are least available to the three-year teacher whose losses are multiplied by 1.82. Under random play in the same re-implementation, that character failed to finish the year in roughly one run in six; the twelve-year teacher failed in between one in thirty and one in forty.

One more decision sits in the ending table. All three results a surviving player can receive carry the same verdict line, '調查結果：查無不當' (index.html:2199-2205). No path finds the teacher at fault. What the complaint decides is how much of the player was left when it arrived, and whether a colleague, a parent or the student came into the room. These figures come from this audit's own re-implementation, not from the game or its author, and were not confirmed by playing every path in a browser.

Verdict

A small, plainly built text game whose rules carry a point of view: caring is charged at a multiple, favours and secrets accrue interest, and the complaint at the end never finds fault but always takes its toll. The writing is the work of someone who has sat in those meetings. The engineering is modest and untested, nothing is saved, and the footer's claim of being offline is contradicted by a visit counter.
